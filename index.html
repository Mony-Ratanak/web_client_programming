Before We Start

What is a Landing Page?

A landing page is designed to get one specific action from the visitor. Every design decision removes distraction and keeps the user focused on that single goal.

❌ Normal WordPress Page

  • Full navigation menu — many links to click away
  • Sidebar with widgets, categories, recent posts
  • Page title printed at the top (ugly for landing pages)
  • Footer with multiple menus and links
  • Goal: encourage browsing
Logo Home · About · Blog · Contact

✅ Landing Page

  • Minimal or no navigation — fewer exits
  • NO sidebar — 100% full width content
  • Page title hidden from visitors
  • Clean footer or no footer at all
  • Goal: ONE conversion action (buy / sign up)

💡 The Golden Rule of Landing Pages: Every extra link on a landing page is a possible exit. When you remove distractions, visitors are forced to either take your desired action or leave — and many more will take the action.

Setting Up Your Blank Canvas

Before adding any content, you must configure the page correctly. A landing page in WordPress requires three specific settings: Full Width template, hidden title, and Draft status until ready.

🔗 Pages vs Posts: In Week 10 you wrote blog Posts. Today you create a Page. Pages don't appear in the blog feed, have no date, and support special templates like Full Width. Pages are for permanent content: About Us, Contact, and Landing Pages.

Phase 2 — Assembling the Layout

0 / 14 steps

Section 1 of 4 — The Hero (Cover Block)

The Hero is the first thing every visitor sees. You have under 3 seconds to communicate your value proposition before they leave. Use a Cover block for the full-width dark hero.

Section 2 of 4 — Social Proof Bar (Trust Logos)

Social proof is the most powerful conversion tool. A row of recognisable logos answers the visitor's unspoken question: "Is this credible?"

Forbes TechCrunch Bloomberg Wired

Section 3 of 4 — The Zig-Zag Features Grid

The Zig-Zag layout alternates Image|Text and Text|Image to keep the user's eye naturally moving down the page. It's the most common layout pattern used by professional landing pages.

Section 4 of 4 — CTA Section & WPForms Integration

The CTA (Call-to-Action) section is where conversions happen. Make it visually distinct — a dark background signals to the user that this is the most important part of the page.

The Gutenberg Advanced CSS Class System

Every Gutenberg block has a hidden superpower in its Advanced settings: the "Additional CSS class(es)" field. Type any class name there and the block gets that class in its HTML output. You can then write CSS targeting that class in Additional CSS.
